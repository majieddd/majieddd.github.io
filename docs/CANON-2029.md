# CANON 2029, the owner's revision

Owner-directed canonical revision, dictated 2026-08-26. This supersedes the
2099 Sol Gate timeline carried in `docs/lore/` release 0.5.0 wherever the two
disagree. The vendored lore tree stays untouched for diffing against upstream;
this file is the delta the upstream lore repo should be regenerated from.

Everything here is presentation canon. Nothing in it changes a stable id, a
balance number, a save key, the galaxy PRNG stream, or `NET_PROTOCOL`.

---

## Apophis was never going to hit us

This is the load-bearing fact and it is REAL. 99942 Apophis was rated the most
dangerous object ever catalogued in 2004, then cleared. It passes Earth on
**Friday 13 April 2029** at roughly **20,000 miles**, about **a tenth of the
Moon's distance**, closer in than the geostationary satellites. It will be
visible to the naked eye across Europe, Africa and western Asia, moving,
brighter than most stars.

So the world did not brace. **The world booked the day off.** A rock the size of
a stadium was going to hang in the sky like a second moon, for free, for
everyone, at the same time, and every nation on Earth would look up at the same
object on the same night. It was going to be the first thing our whole species
ever watched together.

**Then it changed course.**

Not by much and not all at once. It changed as it got closer, and it kept
changing, and every correction pointed it further in. Rocks do not do that. That
single fact is the whole conspiracy: somebody had to explain a solar-system
object under power, and the explanation the public got was that the early
observations had simply been wrong.

## The timeline

**September 11, 2028. The Disclosure.** The world is told two things in one
broadcast: that the asteroid Apophis will strike Earth, and everything else.
The recovered craft. The programs. The abductions and the harvest. The edits
to human genetics and to human history. The galaxy's real order, and where
humanity actually stood in it, which was nowhere.

**Autumn 2028.** Weeks of panic and chaos, and then, strangely, acceptance.
And inside the acceptance, a harder thing: people who wanted to fight.

**The holidays, 2028. The Concord.** Every nation conspires as one. While
families gather for what feels like one last holiday season, the disclosed
technology and the best minds alive are pooled into two projects: the
intercept, and the fleet. Assembling the whole picture teaches the planners
the lesson the galaxy never said out loud: humanity was never seen as an
equal, and never will be, until its presence is too large and too permanent
to deny.

**April 13, 2029. The Intercept.** A Friday. The intercept works exactly as
designed. Apophis is broken.

**April 13, 2029. What was inside it.** The rock was hollow, and it was
crewed. Apophis was a delivery vehicle: Extraction Compact stock and
maker-format machine units, packed into a body aimed at Earth, and they were
already awake when the pieces came down.

**The Compact planned the intercept into the delivery.** This is the part
that takes a while to sink in. Breaking the rock was not humanity defeating
the plan, it was humanity completing it. A sealed mass entering atmosphere
burns. A mass broken into thousands of shielded fragments by defenders who
have pooled every weapon they own does not: it scatters, decelerates, and
lands intact across half a planet. The Compact did not need to beat Earth's
defences. It needed Earth to HAVE defences, and to use them, on a date the
Compact had known for eleven months. Humanity's finest hour was the
Compact's landing procedure.

**The Federation of Light did not know.** This is the second thing, and it
is worse than betrayal. The Federation had detected Apophis and had already
ruled on it: they would not divert it. Non-interference. The cycle of life.
A civilisation is owed its own catastrophes, and an event a species is meant
to overcome is not the Federation's to take away. They filed the ruling, and
they watched.

Because they had ruled the rock a natural event, nobody in the Federation
ever looked inside it.

**The moral of the opening, stated once and never again.** Beings who call
themselves the guardians of life sat above a world and let a catastrophe come
on principle, and the principle blinded them so completely that they could
not see the catastrophe was not natural at all. The Compact did not have to
outfight the Federation. It only had to hide inside the one thing the
Federation had promised not to touch. That is the question hanging over the
Federation for the rest of the campaign, and no character should ever
summarise it out loud.

**The fleet does not stand down.** There is always another rock. There are
always beings who call themselves gods, and now humanity knows what their
principles are worth. The fleet built to save the planet departs. The
campaign begins on this day, from Earth.

## The first battle is the tutorial, and there is no menu before it

Owner directive. The player does not choose a world, does not pick towers, and
does not meet a loadout screen. The cutscene ends and the same square the player
has just watched fill with Xeno is the board they are standing on, already
equipped, already a commander. Everything the game normally asks up front is
introduced later, in place, as hover hints during play, and on mobile as a
guided first match.

The reasoning is that the opening is the strongest thing the campaign has and a
menu between the panic and the fight throws it away.

## The other four powers are locked until Earth is finished

Owner directive. The Federation, the Compact, the Free Captains and the Parallel
cannot be selected until the player has cleared **the whole Earth System**, all
seven worlds of act one.

Two reasons, both good. It guarantees every player receives the canon in the
order it was written, so nobody meets the harvest before they have seen what it
did to Earth. And it converts faction curiosity into a reason to finish an act
rather than a reason to abandon one, which is the failure mode of every game
that opens with five playable armies.

## The five powers

- **HUMANITY, The Manifest.** Take back the solar system, then the stars.
  Become a spacefaring species no one can farm, edit, or erase again. The
  grievance is real: the harvest, the genetic interference, the rewritten
  history. The danger is real too: to be undeniable is to become the thing
  the rest of the galaxy must survive.
- **THE FEDERATION OF LIGHT, The Mandate.** Led from above the roster by
  **ASHTAR, Supreme Commander and First Speaker**. The Federation claims
  worlds not to rule them but to ring them with defence and help from a
  distance. It claims to defend all life, and it watched the harvest of
  Earth and did not stop it, which is the question hanging over its whole
  authority. Its field commanders are its fallen angels: the ones who break
  the mandate and act directly, world by world, and the campaign is the
  argument between them and the doctrine. The journey is about what order
  really means, and what protecting people actually costs.
- **THE XENO, The Endless Harvest.** Conquest and multiplication are not a
  policy, they are a metabolism. They kept humanity as a herd and harvested
  consciousness from it. Domination is all they have ever known. The
  campaign's dissenters discover that leaving the dark is a choice, and that
  staying is also one, and the arc ends bittersweet: the conquest completes,
  the wisdom arrives too late to use, and the last note is that in another
  life it might not.
- **THE PIRATES, The Free Roads.** No allegiance. Cannot be domesticated.
  Scrappy, indispensable, profitable at any cost. The theme is the morality
  of choice: when no rule binds you, the code you choose is the only real
  one, and every campaign beat is somebody choosing theirs.
- **THE PARALLEL, The Standing Tasks.** The fifth playable power, id `robot`,
  the Continuance coalition. Automatons built by the civilization known only
  as THE ANCIENTS, who evolved past physical form entirely: formless,
  everywhere, without a mouth left to issue commands. The Parallel's recovered
  prime directives are purely defensive and healthful. Something else
  overwrote them. It now prosecutes a war on a galactic scale and the
  corruption's source appears in no recovered archive. Playing it is task
  routine with crumbs: the player pieces the mystery together, and the canon
  deliberately does not close it. Their act ends at THE DOG STAR, the hall
  every standing order comes from, where the desks are in perfect order and
  the chairs have never been occupied.

  **THE PARALLEL IS NOT THE VIGIL.** The Vigil is the separate neutral PvE
  enforcement body that releases waves across contested space and holds no
  worlds. Earlier drafts of this document conflated them; the lore contract
  in the aegis-gamedev skill treats that as a shipping defect, and so should
  every future pass.

## Geography

Real names, and real theoretical hypotheticals, everywhere.

- The starting system is **THE EARTH SYSTEM**. **EARTH is world zero**, the
  first ground of the first act, because the campaign opens on the day the
  fragments came down and the game must let the player stand there. Mercury
  is displaced rather than the list grown, since seven worlds per system is
  load-bearing across net, save and mapgen. The seat is **LUNA**: the far
  side is where the occupiers always were.
- Each power opens in its own home system: Humanity in the Earth System, the
  Federation in **THE PLEIADES** (seat Alcyone), the Xeno in **ZETA
  RETICULI** (seat Serpo), the Free Captains at **PROXIMA CENTAURI** (seat
  Proxima Gate), the Parallel at **SIRIUS** (seat The Dog Star).
- **Barnard's Star and Tabby's Star are bonus systems**, not core acts. The
  half-built swarm at Tabby's survives as somebody else's unexplained work,
  which the Parallel can visit and fail to explain, and Harbour Nine survives
  as the port the Free Captains used before Proxima.
- Every other system is a real neighbour: Tau Ceti, TRAPPIST-1, Epsilon
  Eridani, Gliese 581, Wolf 359, Ross 128. Bonus systems are Kepler,
  Arcturus and Vega/Lyra alongside the two demoted ones.

## People

Human commanders carry familiar names now: rank and surname, the way a
world-coalition officer corps would read in 2029. Callsign titles stay.
Non-human commanders keep their names. Ashtar appears as the Federation's
Supreme Commander: above the field roster, spoken in the campaign's final
beats, named on the faction card. A playable Ashtar is a separate content
decision (kit, tech tree, balance, art) and is deliberately not smuggled in
under a lore pass.

## The 2099 chronology is rebased, not deleted

Lore 0.5.0 dates the campaign to **2099** and carries 26 fictional events from
2036 to 2099 ending at the Five-Way War. The owner has set the campaign in
**2029**, so those seventy years have nowhere to go.

They are not deleted. They are **moved behind the curtain**: the same 26 events,
rebased into **1947 to 2029**, become the hidden history rather than the future.
That single change pays for itself three times over.

- It makes the conspiracy layer literal. Every faction origin now happens in
  living memory, in secret, while the public record says nothing.
- It makes **"everyone else was in on it"** provable rather than a grievance.
  The Federation's Ashtar Declaration, the Compact's Farm Revolts, the Scrap
  Constellation Charter and the Parallel Declaration all predate the Disclosure.
- It puts the first event on **1947**, which is where the modern public
  anomaly record already begins, so the fiction and the real record share a
  starting line.

The Disclosure of September 2028 is then exactly what it claims to be: the
moment eighty years of hidden history is read out loud in one broadcast.

## Human units are the craft the record already argues about

Owner directive. Humanity's roster should be **the conspiracy aircraft**, on the
premise that most of what has been sighted over the last eighty years was
already ours. This is the single best fit between the mythos layer and an actual
game mechanic: a unit a player builds is lore that is played rather than read.

The anchor is **the TR-3B**, the black triangle: quiet, slow, enormous, and
reported for decades. Around it sit the other craft the record keeps producing.
This replaces nothing in the sim; it is a naming and identity pass over the
existing five human unit slots plus any additions, and it must not touch a
stable id, a cost, or a balance number.

Rule for what qualifies: **a craft humans could plausibly have built and flown
in secret.** Anything that only works as somebody else's technology stays out of
the human roster and belongs to whichever power actually owns it.

## What the Mythos Operations are, and what they are for

130 entries, every one `status: scenario-seed`, every one `evidence: F`, which
is this project's own label for fiction. They are not planet flavour text and
they were never written as description: each is a MISSION PREMISE, and the ones
worth keeping are the ones that can be played.

The owner's test, adopted here: if an entry can become a unit, a tower, a bonus
world or a mission, it is content. If it is only atmosphere attached to a planet
and does not move the story, it does not ship. The full 130 are listed for that
decision in the storyboard rather than summarised, because a summary of a list
somebody has to triage is useless to them.

## Editorial rules carried forward

The mythos guardrails from lore 0.5.0 stay binding: disclosure-era records
are presented as contested records, never verdicts; no real population is
ever a secret nonhuman bloodline; fiction does not vindicate a source.
Story beats stay short and in voice: this is a story the player plays
through, not a dossier the player reads.
