# Species and Ontologies

The setting rejects the common UFO-lore shortcut in which appearance determines origin, politics, and morality.

Every encountered being is classified on five independent axes:

1. **Morphology:** Grey, humanoid, reptiloid, insectoid, aquatic, avian, amorphous, machine.
2. **Lineage:** biological ancestry or manufacturing genealogy.
3. **Ontology:** organism, clone, synthetic body, remote shell, collective, noetic avatar, information entity.
4. **Origin:** star system, Earth, engineered habitat, parallel state-space, future lineage, unknown.
5. **Polity and alignment:** Human, Federation, Compact, Pirate, Machine, independent; cooperative, coercive, transactional, or unresolved.

## Core archetype families

### Greys

A convergent low-gravity body plan and widely copied wetware platform. Some are citizens, some clones, some remote bodies, and some descendants of extinct original populations.

### Reptiloids and Draco

Reptiloid is morphology. **Draco Hegemony** is a polity containing several species. Reptiloids can serve every faction.

### Mantids

Several eusocial and coordination-oriented lineages share a recognizable body plan. Compact Directorates use collective supervision; Pirate and Federation Mantids often define personhood in ways Humans find difficult to parse.

### Humanlike “Nordics” and contact bodies

Some are genuinely near-human populations. Others are diplomatic avatars selected because Human observers process them with less fear.

### Aquatic and cryptoterrestrial lineages

Some hidden Earth lineages may exist in fiction, but the Old Weather is not secretly biological and does not explain all aquatic encounter traditions.

### Noetic and collective beings

Social-memory complexes, thought-forms, planetary minds, and distributed persons may have no single body. A body encountered in battle can be an interface rather than the being itself.

### Machine avatars

Machines use industrial swarms, ships, ecological symbionts, humanoid shells, and copied organic bodies. Artificial appearance does not prove Machine faction membership.

## Naming rule

Human names such as “Anunnaki,” “Nommo,” “Archon,” or “Pleiadian” may be translations, borrowed mythic labels, broad geography, intelligence codenames, or false identities. A codex must state which.
