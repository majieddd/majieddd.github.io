# Master Chronology - Source History to 2099

This is the chronological backbone used by future dialogue, campaign writing, planet histories, character biographies, and asset dossiers. It intentionally runs three different clocks in one table:

- **A-C history:** established science, authentic records, archaeology, and documented belief history.
- **D-E source history:** traceable fringe claims, contactee/channeling systems, contaminated legends, hoaxes, and conspiracy mutations.
- **F hidden history:** the fictional 2099 universe. F events are not presented as proof that the historical source tradition was correct.

Dates earlier than written history in the hidden-history column are reconstructed in-universe estimates, not exact omniscient dates. Ancient records disagree by design.

## Deep time and the pre-Human layer

| Approximate date | Layer | Event | Why it matters / causal consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| c. 6.4 million BCE | F | The earliest recoverable Source-Lattice engineering epoch begins. | The Lattice is built as infrastructure for transit, identity continuity, collective memory, and noetic synchronization. It makes cooperation cheap but also creates civilizational dependence on shared infrastructure. |
| c. 1.1 million BCE | F | The Lattice reaches a mature interstellar commons. | Civilizations specialize around shared navigation, archives, and collective-mind stabilization instead of duplicating every capability locally. This efficiency later makes the Severance catastrophic. |
| c. 930,000-810,000 BCE | F | The **Severance Crisis** fractures the commons. | The cause remains disputed: attempted privatization, sabotage, voluntary withdrawal, war, and incompatible legal orders all have evidence in different surviving archives. No single faction owns the true story. |
| c. 800,000 BCE | F | Severed civilizations begin emergency substitutes. | Collective minds fragment, identity records fail, and long-distance transit becomes dangerous. Some civilizations accept contraction; others externalize the cost onto dependent worlds. |

A civilization that controls medicine, navigation, identity, reproduction, or cognition can own strategic choices without annexing territory. |

| c. 450,000-300,000 BCE | D/F | Later Anunnaki gold/genetic-worker lore is fictionally echoed by several **Anunnaki Houses** operating concessions in Sol. | The historical extraterrestrial story remains a modern Sitchin-derived interpretation. ## Archaeology, religion, and the mutation of memory

| Date | Layer | Event | Why it matters / causal consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| c. 12,900 years ago | A / disputed | The Younger Dryas begins. A proposed impact/airburst cause remains scientifically contested, with published arguments both for and strongly against the impact hypothesis. | Canon never makes the Younger Dryas an alien attack. It becomes a model for how genuine scientific controversy can be recruited into lost-civilization narratives. |
| 9600-8200 BCE | A | Hunter-gatherer communities build monumental structures at Gobekli Tepe. | This is a control case against reflexive ancient-alien explanations: sophisticated Human organization can appear earlier than modern audiences expect without requiring nonhuman builders. |
| 3rd-1st centuries BCE | C | Enochic Watcher traditions develop themes of heavenly beings, illicit teaching, and dangerous knowledge. | Later ancient-astronaut and forbidden-technology narratives can resemble these motifs, but the source is ancient religious literature, not an extraterrestrial technical report. |
| late 2nd century BCE | A | The Antikythera Mechanism demonstrates sophisticated Human geared astronomical computation. | Another control case: technological surprise is evidence about Human ingenuity before it is evidence for lost high technology or aliens. |
| 2nd-3rd centuries CE | C | Gnostic texts preserve traditions of Archons or cosmic rulers. | Modern alien-parasite readings are later reinterpretations. In fiction, **Archonic** becomes a functional label for administrative intelligences, not proof that ancient Gnostic authors described literal extraterrestrials. |
| 1561 | C | The Nuremberg celestial broadsheet becomes one of many premodern records later reread through UFO categories. | The Translation Horizon rule applies: people describe anomalies using the symbolic and technological vocabulary available to them. |
| 1803 | C | Japanese Utsuro-bune stories describe a strange round vessel, foreign woman, unknown writing, and communication failure. | A valuable source-history analogue for contact filtered through language, folklore, and later UFO expectations. |
| 1882 | C/D | John Ballou Newbrough publishes *Oahspe*, attributed to automatic writing and containing elaborate heavenly administration. | It provides a nineteenth-century precursor for later channeled cosmic bureaucracies, showing that administrative heavens predate the modern UFO age. |
| 1896-1897 | C | The American mystery-airship wave reports craft using the technological vocabulary of the era. | The same sky can be narrated as airship, Zeppelin, rocket, saucer, drone, or anomaly depending on cultural expectations. |
| 1919 | C | Charles Fort publishes *The Book of the Damned*, foregrounding excluded anomalous reports. | The Human Open Archive later borrows the Fortian insight that ignored data can matter while rejecting the inference that exclusion proves an exotic explanation. |

## Chronology locks

1. The playable campaign starts in **2099**. Later dates in old raw research are archival drafts, not binding canon.
2. Planet Nine is a natural astronomical object in canon; the artificial Sol Anchor is a separate installation nearby.
3. No verified historical UAP case is retroactively declared alien by the narrator.
4. Ashtar becomes definitive Federation leader only in the fictional chronology.
5. Old Weather does not explain all USOs, ancient monuments, religious experiences, or premodern sky reports.
6. Source traditions may inspire the hidden history without becoming literal historical transcripts.

These additions do not alter the 2099 causal sequence. They explain why the Archive War contains unusually persuasive fragments of real policy, science, military history, witness testimony, and folklore that can be recombined into false master narratives.
