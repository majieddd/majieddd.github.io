# Disclosed hardware, 2029

Canon: [`../../../CANON-2029.md`](../../../CANON-2029.md). This file is part of
the 2029 revision and is **not** superseded.

It exists to answer one question the campaign asks constantly and the 2099 tree
never had to: **what is humanity actually flying in April 2029, four months
after Disclosure?**

## Where the hardware comes from

The Disclosure of September 11, 2028 was not one nation confessing. Every
nation that had a compartmented aerospace programme had been running it against
the others as much as against anything else. What the broadcast revealed was
not only the recovered craft and the harvest. It was **the real technology
level**: how far ahead of the public record the classified world already was,
and how much of that lead had been spent hiding from other humans.

The Concord of the 2028 holidays is what happens next. Programmes that existed
to out-manoeuvre each other are pooled into two projects, the intercept and the
fleet, because a rock does not care whose flag the airframe carries. **Every
human airframe in the campaign is a Concord airframe: a national black
programme with its compartment walls taken down.**

That is the texture the campaign wants. Human hardware should read as
*recently unified and visibly mismatched*: parts that were never meant to bolt
together, crews trained in different doctrines, and capability that is
genuinely ahead of what any public 2029 could build, because it always was.

## The legends, and the rule about them

The research layer tracks **Solar Warden**, **TR-3B**, and their relatives as
evidence layer **E**, contaminated legends: stories where a real classified
programme, an unrelated field experiment, and a later embellishment have merged
in public memory. See
[`../research/contaminated-legends.md`](../research/contaminated-legends.md).

The editorial rule from lore 0.5.0 stays binding and is not softened here:
**fiction does not vindicate a source.** Cosmic Conquest does not get to say
the legend was right.

What it *can* say, and what is much more interesting, is the shape Disclosure
actually takes:

> The legend was contaminated. It was also pointing at something.

So the canon position on any famous programme name is:

- **The name is a public artefact**, not an official designation. It is what
  the outside called a thing it could only see the edges of.
- **The disclosed article is adjacent to the legend, never identical to it.**
  Something in that hangar was real. It was not the thing the story described,
  and the difference is usually the interesting part.
- **Crews use the public name anyway**, with the weariness of people who know
  it is wrong and have given up correcting it. That is how real aircrews talk
  about the names outsiders give their aircraft.

This is not a hedge. It is the sharper version. A craft that turns out to be
exactly what the forums said is a story with nothing left in it; a craft that
is *nearly* that, and stranger in a direction nobody guessed, is a story that
still has a scene in it.

## Content decision, deliberately not taken here

Turning any of this into a **playable unit** (a new id, a kit, a place in the
muster roster, balance numbers, art) is a content decision with a cost, and
`CANON-2029.md` already draws that line for the Ashtar case:

> A playable Ashtar is a separate content decision (kit, tech tree, balance,
> art) and is deliberately not smuggled in under a lore pass.

The same applies to a disclosed-craft unit. This file establishes what such a
unit would BE and what it may and may not claim, so that the decision can be
taken deliberately, with the guardrail already settled, rather than argued
again at implementation time.

**If it is taken**, the shape that fits the existing Human roster is an air
frame: the current human units are ground (trooper, linebreaker, dragoon,
vanguard) with one light fast body (gunskiff), and the disclosed-craft slot is
the obvious air unit humanity should not plausibly have in 2029 and visibly
does.

**What it must not do:** carry a real programme name as its official
designation in a way that reads as the game asserting the programme existed as
described. A callsign, a crew nickname, or a Concord designation with the
public name attached as *what people call it* all satisfy the guardrail. A
unit literally named after a contaminated legend, presented as canon fact, does
not.
