# Campaign Era: 2099

The playable campaign begins in **2099**, at the opening of the Five-Way War. This page is the game-facing version of the larger [Hidden History](hidden-history.md).

## What one campaign represents

A generated galaxy is not the entire Milky Way. It is a **Source-Lattice pentad**: five star systems whose surviving transit, memory, and jurisdiction records still recognize one another. Each system contains seven registered world-slots because the original Lattice used a seven-part civil schema:

1. settlement and habitation;
2. defense;
3. production;
4. navigation;
5. archive;
6. biosphere or resource stewardship;
7. command authority.

Modern worlds rarely preserve the original function cleanly. A dead archive can become a fortress; an ecological node can become a mine; a command seat can be occupied by a Pirate market. The campaign generator recombines these inherited functions into a new theater while keeping the underlying five-by-seven structure deterministic.

## Why every system has a commander seat

The final world in a system is a **charter seat**. Holding it allows a faction commander to speak for the system inside the Lattice, even when that authority is contested locally. Defeating the commander does not prove moral ownership. It breaks the current command claim and lets the victor submit a replacement record.

## Why two worlds are contested

Two worlds in every system carry overlapping valid claims. Their three-way battlefields are not random escalation; they are jurisdictions where two rival command records remain active while the player files a third. The Lattice cannot decide politically, so it exposes all claimants to the same neutral enforcement and accepts the survivor.

## Why the Vigil attacks everyone

The **Vigil** is legacy Old Weather enforcement. It recognizes lane boundaries, threat thresholds, and ancient safety rules, but not current flags. A Human colony, Federation protectorate, Compact concession, Pirate sanctuary, and Vigil foundry can all be unauthorized in different ways.

The neutral wave is therefore not wildlife and not a secret Pirate army. It is a jurisdiction defending itself according to rules whose legislature is extinct.

## Why kills become attacks

Contested nodes run the **Echo Reversal Protocol**. When a hostile body is destroyed, the node captures a short-lived combat-state record and reconstructs an unstable echo. That echo retraces the victor's lane, crosses the neutral zone, and advances into the rival jurisdiction. A second death destroys the unstable record permanently.

This is why kills pressure the rival, why echoes are faster and frailer, and why the Ossuary can disable reanimation: it is a state-deletion jurisdiction that refuses to preserve combat records.

## Campaign act structure

| Act | Player experience | Lore revelation |
|---|---|---|
| **I - The First Claim** | Choose a banner, secure entry worlds, rescue the first unit. | The map is a legal and infrastructural record, not empty territory. |
| **II - The Archive War** | Encounter contradictory dossiers, terrain nodes, and rival doctrines. | Pre-2099 UAP history contains mundane error, disinformation, and genuine contacts from different operators. |
| **III - The Five Seats** | Defeat system commanders and learn their private motives. | No faction's public explanation of the war is complete. |
| **IV - The Mirror Trial** | Discover and unlock the Vigil after a conquered galaxy. | Machines are political persons; the Vigil is not their obedient army. |
| **V - The Maelstrom** | Survive a many-seat jurisdictional overload. | The Source Lattice can be restored, privatized, quarantined, opened, or allowed to remain fragmented. |

## Stars, SOULS, and rescues

- **One star** means the node accepted the victory record. The player receives progression and **SOULS**, Source-Oriented Unified Lattice Signatures.
- **Two stars** means the population, garrison, or local archive survived in a recoverable state. A faction unit can be rescued, recruited, contracted, copied, or lawfully transferred.
- **Three stars** means control was stable enough to recover the world's strategic boon without destroying the system that produced it.

## The campaign question

Each faction can win a battle. The campaign asks which definition of conquest becomes ordinary:

- Humans **Settle**.
- The Federation **Accords**.
- Xenos **Bind**.
- Pirates **Route**.
- Machines **Continue**.

A world can be subject to all five at once. The banner on the map shows who currently controls the battle record, not the whole truth of the world.
