# Inside Job Reference Map

*Inside Job* is useful here as a **fictional design corpus**, not a source of evidence. Creator Shion Takeuchi has described the series in terms of loss of agency, social distrust, and the comic idea that a shadow government would be as bureaucratic and fallible as any other workplace. The show ran for 18 episodes across two released parts. Its public episode summaries provide a compact catalogue of conspiracy motifs, but this page uses only short paraphrases and structural lessons; it does not reproduce dialogue or detailed plots.

## Rules for using the corpus

1. Import institutional logic, not copyrighted characters or plots.
2. Give every hidden system a budget, owner, staff, maintenance burden, failure mode, and succession problem.
3. Make conspiracies compete. A world with several partially informed institutions is richer than one controlled by an omniscient cabal.
4. Keep the real source genealogy beside the fictional motif. A satirical reptoid gala is not evidence for reptilian rulers; it is a prompt to design alien diplomacy and prejudice.
5. Personal incentives matter. The operator of an impossible machine can still be lonely, ambitious, tired, corrupt, ethical, or wrong.

## What this corpus contributes that a conspiracy list does not

### Institutional ecology

The show repeatedly treats hidden organizations as employers and contractors rather than disembodied evil. In the 2099 canon, this becomes the **Six Hidden Tables**: overlapping institutions that exchange services, compete for jurisdiction, inherit old obligations, and often misread one another.

### Maintenance as plot

Reality editors, memory systems, artificial leaders, hidden colonies, and biological elites all generate upkeep. Maintenance creates workers, records, supply chains, vulnerabilities, whistleblowers, procurement scandals, and accidents. This is the most transferable design insight for commanders, towers, planets, and campaign events.

### Sincere paternalism

The final governance motif is especially relevant to the Federation and Population Ledger. A hidden council may sincerely believe it protects civilization. Sincerity does not create consent, and competence does not create legitimate authority.

### Branch-history consequences

The reality-editing premise is strongest when translated into the established four-layer time model:

- **Retrocognition** reconstructs what happened.
- **Information backflow** moves data without moving bodies.
- **Branch selection** produces competing histories and archive forks.
- **Physical chronology transit** is rare, expensive, and identity-destabilizing.

This keeps alternate-history storytelling compatible with saves, evidence chains, and multiplayer determinism.
