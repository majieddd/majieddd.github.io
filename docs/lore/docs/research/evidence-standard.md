# Evidence Standard

Real-world research and fictional canon share a repository but not an evidentiary category.

## Labels

- **A — Established core:** verified science, current official status, or directly verifiable publication fact.
- **B — Authentic record:** genuine government or archival record whose event attribution may remain unresolved.
- **C — Historical tradition:** documented religious text, folklore, movement, or academic history.
- **D — Traceable fringe claim:** named contactee, channeling, ancient-astronaut, experiencer, or conspiracy literature.
- **E — Contaminated claim:** anonymous leak, forgery, admitted hoax, circular sourcing, severe provenance failure, or composite legend.
- **F — Fictional synthesis:** original *Cosmic Conquest* canon.

## Seven questions for every source

1. What does the source actually say?
2. What does the source’s existence establish?
3. What does it not establish?
4. What is the earliest accessible version?
5. How did later retellings mutate it?
6. Which credible sources conflict with it?
7. What fictional function can be adapted without misrepresenting the evidence?

## Seven scores for UAP case research

Keep these separate:

1. Provenance
2. Witness chain
3. Sensor chain
4. Independent corroboration
5. Conventional analysis
6. Residual anomaly
7. Evidence specifically supporting extraordinary attribution

A case can have authentic provenance and weak extraordinary attribution. A case can remain unidentified while its apparent behavior is resolved.

## Two-Key Rule

Before a campaign deliberately resolves a mystery, maintain at least two viable interpretations—normally one conventional and one speculative.

Examples:

- A video can be authentic while the object is ordinary.
- A forged memo can copy a real compartmentation practice.
- A spiritual experience can be sincere while its institution is captured.
- An Old Weather artifact can exist without explaining every historical USO report.
- Planet Nine can exist without being Nibiru or an artificial gate.

## Source hierarchy

Prefer primary science, official records, original historical texts, academic scholarship, first editions, and direct movement archives. Use mirrors when necessary. Treat anonymous material as folklore rather than authority.
