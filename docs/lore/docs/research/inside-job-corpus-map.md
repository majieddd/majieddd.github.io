# Inside Job Design Corpus Map

## Why it belongs in the research stack

*Inside Job* is useful as a **fictional design corpus**, not evidence. Its most valuable move is to treat global conspiracies as workplace systems staffed by people with budgets, rivalries, emotions, maintenance burdens, and career incentives. That is the opposite of an omniscient cabal.

Only episode titles and high-level design observations are used here. Scripts, dialogue, scene recreation, and proprietary characters are not copied.

## The ten transferable principles

### Bureaucratic friction

Hidden power is divided by forms, budgets, approvals, staffing, maintenance, and turf wars.

### Budget test

Every conspiracy consumes labor, facilities, supply chains, data storage, training, and political capital.

### Jurisdiction test

Secret offices overlap, contradict, and sometimes sabotage one another because no one has universal authority.

### Succession test

A hidden system must explain who inherits command keys, memories, liabilities, and legitimacy.

### Procurement test

Impossible technology still needs materials, contractors, test failures, repair, and disposal.

### Error budget

Coverups fail through ordinary mistakes, personality conflicts, and poorly specified objectives.

### Partial truth

A false public story may protect a real but different program; the true part should not authenticate the false whole.

### Human motive

Characters need love, fear, status, guilt, ideology, grief, or ambition beyond faction doctrine.

### One-percent-better rule

A vast institution is believable when it improves outcomes marginally, not when it flawlessly controls everything.

### Comedy boundary

Satire can remix harmless or fictional motifs, but must not launder scapegoating or dehumanizing conspiracy claims into canon.

## The Cognito Principle

Before any covert institution enters canon, answer all of the following:

1. What is its mandate?
2. Who funds it?
3. Who works there and why?
4. What does it procure and repair?
5. Where does its jurisdiction end?
6. Who audits it?
7. How does succession work?
8. Which rival office wants its budget?
9. What is its ordinary error rate?
10. What happens when it fails publicly?

An organization that cannot answer those questions is a rumor, not a usable faction institution.
