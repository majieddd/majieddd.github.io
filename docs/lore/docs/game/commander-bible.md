# Commander Bible

The 26 live commander IDs are preserved. This bible changes context and voice, not mechanics or save data.

## Character rule

Every commander needs four things visible in play:

1. a historical reason their mechanical trait exists;
2. a present objective in the system being contested;
3. an internal contradiction that can change across a campaign;
4. a vocabulary distinct from their faction stereotype.

Ashtar remains the Federation's supreme leader but is not one of the ordinary field-command slots. System commanders are powerful regional actors, not the sole leaders of their factions.

## The Vigil's Mirror Trials

`NYX-R`, `LUMEN-R`, `MAWLORD-R`, and `DREGG-R` are not literal resurrected commanders and not stolen souls. They are **Mirror Trials**: autonomous minds initialized from combat telemetry, doctrinal records, and adversarial models of their namesakes. Their resemblance is the political problem.

The Vigil argues that a model which diverges, remembers, and accepts responsibility is a person. Opponents argue that the Vigil built commanders from data it did not have permission to use. The game can preserve familiar mechanical identities while making every Machine commander a person with a different answer.

## Cadre

`cadre` is the **Interoperability Cadre**, created before the player swears a banner. Cadre is allowed to field every origin because its job is to teach common interfaces and identify where doctrines cannot be reconciled. It is intentionally less mythic than the faction commanders and becomes more poignant as the player learns how political every piece of equipment actually is.
