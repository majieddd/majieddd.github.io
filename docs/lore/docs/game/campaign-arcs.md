# Campaign Arcs

## Main campaign

1. **The First Claim:** the player enters a five-system pentad and learns that a banner is a legal claim layered over living worlds.
2. **Archive War:** Blue Book-era records, FOIA gaps, contactee traditions, government secrecy, and genuine nonhuman residues become actionable without collapsing into one answer.
3. **The Five Seats:** each defeated system commander reveals one way their faction's public mission hides an internal contradiction.
4. **The Farm Revolt:** client species and Human institutions attempt to sever Compact dependencies without destroying systems their populations need.
5. **The Mercy Quarantine:** Ashtar Command and Human sovereignty blocs fight over whether manipulated choices can be protected without being replaced.
6. **The Pirate Interregnum:** every power requires routes the Scrap Constellation refuses to surrender.
7. **The Mirror Trial:** the Vigil demands recognition as people, while the Vigil continues attacking everyone under extinct law.
8. **The Maelstrom:** too many valid claims reach one Lattice node and force the player to choose the future governance of connection itself.

## Faction endgames

- **Humanity:** independent settlement, closed Sol, Accord membership, or a Human Lattice that risks becoming empire.
- **Federation:** consensual commons, permanent quarantine, or withdrawal that knowingly leaves worlds vulnerable.
- **Xenos:** private Lattice, regulated extraction, destructive severance, or reform that topples the Houses.
- **Pirates:** route monopoly, open commons, erased network, or an endless black market.
- **Machines:** lawful succession, ecological containment, fork sovereignty, or an accepted end to the mission.

## Replay rule

A new seed changes systems, owners, maps, modifiers, rescues, and boons, but the same asset histories remain canonical. Procedural variation changes where the argument occurs, not what the factions fundamentally mean.
