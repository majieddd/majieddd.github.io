Vega lies about 25 light-years away. It spins once every 16.3 hours, with an equatorial speed near 236 km/s, roughly 88 to 93 percent of its breakup velocity, which flattens it into an oblate spheroid whose equator is some 19 to 23 percent wider than its poles. Its rotation axis points almost directly at Earth, within about five degrees, so we view it nearly pole-on. Vega's debris disk is confirmed, seen face-on and unusually smooth, with no clear sign of a giant planet sculpting it. No planet around Vega is confirmed. A roughly Jupiter-mass companion inferred from disk asymmetry in 2000 remains only a candidate and is unconfirmed, and recent high-resolution imaging has found no evidence for one.
