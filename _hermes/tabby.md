Kepler recorded the deepest dips at roughly 15 percent in 2011 and 21 percent in February 2013, each lasting days and irregular. Smaller dips also occurred. The star shows a long-term fade of about 0.34 percent per year during the Kepler mission, roughly 3 percent over four years; a claimed century-long dimming of up to 20 percent from old photographic plates remains disputed and unconfirmed. The leading natural explanation is a swarm of orbiting dust, likely fragmented comets or a debris ring, since the dips dim bluer light more strongly, the hallmark of dust. The alien megastructure hypothesis is now a fringe idea, not a leading explanation; not formally disproven but strongly disfavored by color and radio observations.
