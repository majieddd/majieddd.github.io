Kepler-186f has about 1.11 Earth radii, a 130-day orbit, and circles the M1 red dwarf Kepler-186 near its habitable zone's outer edge; the cool, flaring M-dwarf and likely tidal locking weaken its habitability claim. Kepler-442b is roughly 1.34 Earth radii, orbits in 112 days around the K-type orange dwarf Kepler-442, and carries the strongest habitable-zone claim of the three with the highest habitability index (ESI near 0.84). Kepler-452b is about 1.63 Earth radii with a 385-day year around a sun-like G2V star, good for star similarity, but its size hints at a possible gaseous composition and its mass remains unconfirmed, so its rocky-habitable status is unconfirmed. Kepler-442b holds the highest habitability index.
