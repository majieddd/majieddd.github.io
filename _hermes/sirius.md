Sirius A is spectral type A1V, a blue-white main-sequence star about 25 times as luminous as the Sun. Sirius B is a carbon-oxygen white dwarf: its progenitor was roughly 5 to 6 solar masses, but it lost most of that and now holds about 1.02 solar masses compressed into a radius of roughly 0.0084 solar radii, with surface gravity near log g 8.57 in cgs units. White-dwarf core crystallization is real physics and is now observationally confirmed in general, but it only occurs once a dwarf cools to a few thousand kelvin. At its current ~25,000 K and young ~126-million-year cooling age, Sirius B's core has not yet frozen. Calling it a "diamond star" is journalism, not astronomy; that nickname belongs to cooler, crystallized dwarfs like BPM 37093.
