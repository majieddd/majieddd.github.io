Barnard's Star has the largest known proper motion of any star, about 10.4 arcseconds per year, notable because it is the nearest single star to the Sun, so its apparent drift across the sky is the fastest. Four sub-Earth planets are now confirmed through ESPRESSO and MAROON-X radial velocity: b at 3.15 days, c at 4.12 days, d at 2.34 days, and e at 6.74 days, with minimum masses ranging from about 0.19 to 0.34 Earth masses. Two earlier detections were retracted: Peter van de Kamp's 1960s astrometric Jupiter-mass planet was an artifact of telescope lens adjustments, and the 2018 super-Earth candidate of about 3.2 Earth masses on a 233-day orbit was refuted in 2021 as stellar activity linked to the star's rotation.
